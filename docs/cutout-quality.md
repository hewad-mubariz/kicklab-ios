# Foreground quality: person, feet and ball

Cutout quality takes priority over zoom controls and further stadium art changes.

Latest follow-up: [matting strategy selection](matting-strategy-selection.md) records the implemented full-frame motion envelope, local person repair, foreground color refinement, motion-aware alpha filtering, 57 passing tests, and fresh iPhone verification. It also compares native alternatives and fixed/dynamic RVM. The [earlier person-boundary audit](person-boundary-audit.md) isolated defects upstream of cleanup. Availability checks alone do not establish boundary quality; remaining edge flicker is not considered solved universally.

## Findings from the real clips

The original park clip is 2160 × 3840 at roughly 60 fps. The previous preview reduced it to 720 × 1280 before segmentation. That discarded useful source detail before Vision saw the hands, bare feet and ball. The one-second calibration crop could also clip a fast extended foot. The person mask could include a detached ball, so taking the maximum of that mask and a tighter ball mask retained the larger mask's background fringe.

Three native Vision mask families were compared at identical timestamps and crops, both at 720p and at source resolution: accurate person segmentation, person instance masks, and foreground instance masks. Foreground instance masks with the source-resolution input gave the most useful result in this local visual comparison. This is not a claim of benchmark superiority across all videos.

The park's planted toes are partly occluded by grass in the original video. A better matte can preserve visible details but cannot reconstruct those hidden toes. The second clip also contains genuine motion blur on the kicking foot and ball; a truthful matte retains that coverage rather than inventing a sharp contour.

Full-sequence review also found weak ball detections landing on foliage beside the real ball, notably around 4.63 seconds. Sharpening a mask around that box preserves the wrong object. Separately, a wide ball crop sometimes made subject lifting select the adjacent torso and omit the football, triggering an overly loose contour fallback.

The later device recording reproduced the user's foliage-filled circle at frame 124 (4.133 seconds) even though a Mac-only check of that frame had looked correct. Vision's generic object tracker could drift while retaining a high confidence. The cutout also applied 0.15/0.40 confidence gates to a detector calibrated to admit scores down to 0.05. In a sequential 212-frame Mac baseline, those gates removed the separate ball matte in 14 frames around 5.63–6.27 seconds. All video frames still decoded: this was missing foreground content, not dropped encoded video frames.

The indoor clip starts with an empty scene and a person walking into and out of frame. Unqualified subject lifting selected furniture in those empty frames. A person-specific guide now determines which foreground instance is eligible; it does not soften the selected instance's high-resolution boundary. The ball cutout also requires a visible person, preventing the detector's round-toy false positive in the empty room from initializing a foreground. Consequently, standalone ball footage with no visible person is outside this preview's current supported case.

## Implemented

- The prepared preview decodes an upright SDR source at up to 2160p short edge for segmentation, then produces an up-to-1080p preview with Lanczos downsampling. Scene preparation now covers the full clip at up to 30 fps for export; the cutout algorithm is unchanged by that integration.
- `DetailedForegroundMaskProcessor` uses `VNGenerateForegroundInstanceMaskRequest` and its scaled mask. Calibration now builds the person crop from all preparation frames. Accurate person segmentation guides instance identity and repairs connected local missing regions, including small hands that passed the former whole-body/lower-third checks. If no eligible instance is returned, the guide supplies the fallback matte. Whole missing limbs are repaired from current-frame evidence; a later edge filter uses motion-validated history near the current boundary.
- `BallCutoutTracker` now uses `BallImageLocator` instead of the generic Vision object tracker. Detector observations down to the model's 0.05 threshold and short motion predictions propose search areas. Current-frame circular boundary support, learned ball color/chroma, and a rolling radius estimate select the location. Detector confidence cannot independently authorize a foreground patch, and a prediction without image support returns no ball. Counting and the stored track are unchanged.
- The ball receives a separate subject lift at the verified location. A tight boundary allowance prevents the selected instance from spilling across adjacent grass or a foot. A contour fallback additionally requires a positive localization ranking score of at least 10; weak matches must be confirmed by subject lifting. It no longer performs another independent, wider center search. The action cache includes low-confidence observations too, so a recovered ball is not clipped by an earlier high-confidence-only crop.
- Numeric alpha is normalised without gamma conversion. A source-guided boundary pass can correct incorrectly opaque fringes as well as soft edges. Version-2 caches store corrected, linear-premultiplied foreground color for consistent preview, export and floor reflections. Motion-aware alpha history is applied before encoding.
- Ground contact uses the bottom edge of the connected visible sole, including meaningful soft coverage, rather than averaging rows above it. The existing contact stability filter and camera geometry remain in place.
- Replay immediately requests a redraw when the edit changes. `None` explicitly bypasses emission, particles and cached bloom. A shader guard also prevents off-state glow from being resolved. Independently selected ball materials and environment grading still work.

Fire persisting after None was not reproduced in the existing simulator build: switching the same paused frame between Fire and None visibly removed it. The new explicit off path hardens that behavior; the report should still be checked against the user's specific device interaction rather than claiming the original cause has been conclusively identified.

The updated Replay & Effects UI was also checked on the prepared SDR source at approximately 4.99 seconds: Fire was visible while paused and choosing None removed it immediately. This complements the GPU regression test; it does not establish the original reported trigger.

## Tool comparison and recommendation

| Option | Fit for KickLab |
|---|---|
| [Apple Vision subject lifting](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10176/) | Current implementation: native deployment, source-aware scaled masks, and a separate crop for the ball. Image-based, so temporal consistency still needs testing on a broader set of clips. |
| [OpenCV GrabCut](https://docs.opencv.org/4.13.0/d8/d83/tutorial_py_grabcut.html) | Useful interactive initialization/refinement, not an automatic semantic person-and-ball video matte. |
| [OpenCV alphamat](https://docs.opencv.org/4.12.0/d4/d40/group__alphamat.html) / [global-matting C++](https://github.com/atilimcetin/global-matting) | Can refine an uncertain boundary given a foreground/background/unknown trimap. The C++ implementation is MIT. Neither supplies object identity or temporal tracking by itself. Not added as a dependency without a measured advantage over the native cut. |
| [Robust Video Matting](https://github.com/PeterL1n/RobustVideoMatting) | A temporal human-matting baseline with official Core ML exports. Person-focused; the ball still needs separate handling. Repository is GPL-3.0, so not treated as a drop-in proprietary-app dependency. |
| [MatAnyone 2](https://github.com/pq-yang/MatAnyone2) / [MatAnyone2Kit](https://github.com/flowtyone/MatAnyone2Kit) | Strong candidate for a dedicated video-matting benchmark. The Swift port reports 30 fps on A18 at 288 × 512 working resolution; that is its published result, not a KickLab measurement. The port is GPL-3.0 and bundled weights use S-Lab's non-commercial license. It is not bundled in KickLab. A commercial route needs suitable model rights and validation on juggling footage. |

Recommended path: keep evaluating the native refinement in the prepared preview, with current-frame ball identity validation and a separate ball mask. OpenCV optical flow or a trimap matting library would not independently solve a tracker selecting foliage. Evaluate a suitably licensed temporal matting model against held-out juggling clips if remaining edge flicker warrants it. A person-only model should not be assumed to preserve an airborne football. The existing KickLab detector supplies ball proposals; replacing its detector is not the same as adding a matting model.

All footage and masks in this evaluation were processed locally. No clips were sent to an external background-removal service.

## Verification artifacts

`artifacts/ball-cutout-fix/` contains the new sequential regression review. `scripts/review-ball-cutout.swift` processes the actual source frames in order and saves the original/matte comparison and per-frame JSON. The park baseline had 14 missing ball mattes; the revised Mac sequence has zero across 212 frames, all using subject lifting. That count is an availability check, not a segmentation-accuracy score. The 42-test suite includes current-pixel recovery through a missing detection, rejection of a high-confidence foliage distractor, withholding a predicted circle after the ball disappears, lower-body coverage loss, numeric alpha, ground contact, a shaded ball against brighter foliage, lighting changes that lower the ranking score without invalidating the boundary, and the existing effect tests.

Every newly prepared preview also saves `cutout-frames.json` beside `scene.json`: exact presentation time, person/ball presence, verified ball bounds, mask method and localization quality. These diagnostics make device-specific failures traceable without relying on a single paused screenshot.

The final device rerun is in `artifacts/ball-cutout-fix/iphone-final/`: 212 frames, no missing person or ball matte, and all 212 ball mattes produced by subject lifting. The foliage error at frame 124 and the later near-head pass were checked on actual iPhone output. `comparison-4.133.png` shows the old phone result on the left and the corrected phone result on the right. All four movies fully decode, retain AAC audio, and contain 212 video frames. The installed phone build uses the real native pipeline. Simulator playback uses these same phone files with the explicit “Prepared iPhone sample” label; a paused seek to 4.133 seconds displays the corrected ball.

The 360-frame indoor check (`indoor-v4/`) has no missing separate ball matte during the active juggling section from 6–12 seconds and no ball patch in empty-person frames. Entry/exit and held, partly offscreen balls still require distinction from airborne-ball misses. Low-scoring geometric fallback patches seen during an offscreen exit are now withheld unless subject lifting confirms them; the final guard does not change the park sequence, which uses subject lifting throughout. This remains a two-clip regression review, not a general accuracy benchmark.

`artifacts/cutout-refinement/` contains the earlier Vision comparisons, before/after crops, full preview renders, and diagnostics. `scripts/compare-cutout-models.swift` reproduces the mask comparison. The verification below describes that earlier pass, not proof that it solved the later reported tracker drift.

The final optimized development build was installed on the connected iPhone 17 Pro Max. The phone completed the original 4K park source as three 1080 × 1920 movies, 212 frames / 7.04 seconds. All three were copied back, fully decoded without errors, and checked for AAC audio. See `device-status.json`, `media-verification.json`, and `iphone-verified/`. A separate 12-second, 360-frame indoor render checks entry/exit, empty-room frames, toes and the moving ball (`indoor-verified/`). The simulator's final stadium walkthrough uses the actual iPhone files with the explicit “Prepared iPhone sample” label; Vision inference was not claimed to run in the simulator.

The final shared preparer's cancellation check also passed with real Vision/Metal processing: no completed preview was returned and partial files were removed (`cancellation.log`).

For representative CPU processing speed, the installed development build used the command-line `SWIFT_OPTIMIZATION_LEVEL=-O` override. The repository's Debug build configuration was not changed. This short run is not a thermal or sustained-performance benchmark.

Limitations: the dominant-person selection is not a multi-person identity tracker. The ball search is a local appearance-and-boundary method, not a newly trained semantic video model; similarly colored round distractors, strong blur and long occlusions still need broader testing. Fully offscreen/occluded balls are not fabricated. A partly offscreen ball may be retained by the person mask when held, but separate-ball localization currently requires a complete visible circle. Image-based masks can still flicker, and the contour fallback is an approximation. This is a bounded prepared preview, not a claim of live real-time matting or production-quality foreground extraction.
