# Stadium look-around preview

Open Replay & Effects → Environment → Classic Stadium. The night bowl now has photographed-style architecture, roof floodlights, warm concourse lighting, native KICKLAB screens and continuous fascia. The camera places the player closer, with the visible supporting sole on the turf and the screens above the player.

Drag the picture or use the left/right buttons to look around the full stadium. Rotation and zoom ease toward the requested view using shortest-path yaw; Reduce Motion applies changes directly. Reset view returns to the player and restores the zoom. The zoom slider changes field of view. Playback controls sit below the picture so they do not cover the feet. On an iPhone, enable Look around with phone to use relative device attitude; the starting phone orientation becomes the origin. Reset also recentres that origin. Device motion stops when the preview leaves the screen. The environment surrounds the camera; rotating away lets the player leave the frame. This is not a reconstructed 3D body or an orbit behind the person.

Follow recorded camera uses Vision translation registration on a fixed background strip outside the calibrated person crop. It estimates pan and tilt, rejects large jumps, and limits cumulative motion to ±0.25 radians. Failed registration holds the last estimate. This is an approximation: it does not recover camera translation, parallax, roll or lens zoom. More demanding handheld clips need a separate camera solve or capture-time pose recording. Phone look-around uses [Core Motion device attitude](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/getting-processed-device-motion-data) with the [arbitrary-horizontal, vertical-Z reference frame](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmattitudereferenceframe/xarbitraryzvertical).

Preparation retains up to 2160-pixel short-edge source detail. A fixed action crop includes the person and observed ball path, then caches color and numeric alpha side by side in one H.264 frame. Each half is capped at 1080 × 1920. Color and matte therefore share a presentation timestamp during playback and seeking. `scene.json` stores the crop, calibrated camera, per-frame foot contact and camera pan. The interactive Metal renderer decodes that cache and redraws the scene without repeating Vision. The original and pre-rendered locked/moving comparisons remain available for verification and older fixtures.

Preparation covers the full clip at up to 30 fps; source audio is preserved. **Use Classic Stadium** applies the selected view to replay and Edited Save / Share, including the touch counter. See [environment-export.md](environment-export.md). Distant architecture is a generated texture projected onto the bowl, not individually modelled seats or roof trusses. Asset paths and prompts are in [stadium-360-assets.md](stadium-360-assets.md).

## Verification

- 38 tests passed, including source/view camera projection, planted foot contact, full-turn camera orientation, rejected registration jumps, packed alpha bytes, and timestamp metadata round trips.
- Signed iPhone and simulator builds succeeded. The connected iPhone prepared the real 7.04-second, 212-frame outdoor clip. All four movies decoded without errors and retained audio; comparison movies are 1080 × 1920 and the native action cache is 1912 × 1254 (956 × 1254 per half).
- A known (+5, +3)-pixel translation passed the Vision camera-registration check in `scripts/check-stadium-motion.swift`. The outdoor sample is stationary and does not independently validate a moving handheld camera.
- Front, side and rear views were rendered from the prepared cache. The 0° and 360° outputs differ by a mean 0.0029 of 255 per channel (floating-point rounding), with no visible wrap jump.
- Cancelling the four-output preparation removed every partial movie/cache and returned no completed result (`cancellation.log`).
- Evidence: `artifacts/stadium-360/media-verification.json`, `motion-verification.log`, `tests.log`, `iphone-camera/`, and `iphone-verified/`. The simulator uses that actual iPhone result and labels it Prepared iPhone sample because its Vision runtime cannot run this cutout request.

The simulator walkthrough verified the left/right camera controls, Reset, zoom, seeking, playback and paused Original/Stadium comparison. Video output is attached before the initial seek so paused Original/Stadium comparisons receive a decoded frame. Automated simulator dragging did not reliably trigger even its native slider, so touch dragging and physical phone rotation still need hands-on testing. Sustained thermal/frame-rate performance also remains unmeasured. No 30-fps performance claim is made from the display-link target alone.
