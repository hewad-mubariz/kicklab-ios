REVISION 2 REPEATABILITY: A REPLAY DEFECT CORRECTED; LIVE CONSISTENCY STILL NEEDS WORK

Completed 30 valid iPhone 17 Pro Max runs: 16 alternating rule comparisons on build 2026103017,
then 14 clock/rule comparisons on build 2026103018. All stayed nominal, with zero processing overflow,
zero late RF replies, exact saved-counter replay and matched source hashes. One stalled preparation/replay
attempt produced no count, was preserved under failed/, and was excluded before its successful retry.

WHAT WAS CORRECTED
The file-replay harness started its clock before duration loading and first-frame decoding finished.
It also used Task.sleep with unspecified timing tolerance. In raw-juggling's original opening gap,
one measured wake was 130.615 ms late, despite decoding that frame taking only 0.307 ms. That skipped four
source frames. Another old-clock wake was 32.002 ms late: no drop, but substantial delivery jitter.

The explicit --replay-precise-clock-v1 mode anchors time after the first decoded frame and requests zero
sleep tolerance. It retains the one-frame lateness/drop limit and never catches up with stale frames.
The corresponding first long-gap wake in the measured precise control was 1.082 ms late. All 10 precise
replays had zero skipped source frames. Source-admission logs agree exactly with the trace's delivered
source-frame identities. This is a DEBUG replay correction, not a camera-processing or model change.
Zero requested tolerance remains a scheduling request, not a real-time guarantee; actual lateness is logged.

WHERE THE REMAINING VARIATION OCCURS
With corrected playback, raw pair 1's ball inputs first differ at 4.635 s. Control performed a failed retry
on the older 4.368 s image and next ran regular detection at 4.668 s; the other arm ran regular detection at
4.635 s. The regular clock is reset by an admitted impact-gap retry, even when that retry finds no usable ball.
IMG 8606 pair 1 first changes ball inputs at 9.571 s after an impact-gap retry on 9.305 s: regular sampling resumes
at 9.571 s versus 9.605 s. Shared asynchronous scheduling remains variable even with identical delivered frames.

A prior paired-run example provides a complete count consequence: at 4.935 s a failed retry postponed
regular sampling from 5.201 s to 5.235 s. The 4.901 s contact counted when the ball returned at 5.201 s, but was
reported trackLost when it returned one frame later. Evidence is saved in retry-clock-proof.json.
This code is shared by paced replay and live recording, so the scheduling sensitivity is not confined to
the replay harness. These tests are original-video replays, not repeated physical camera recordings;
they do not quantify its frequency during actual juggling.

IMG 8606 also retains a separate revision 2 limitation: the raw pose request sometimes returns no person.
Fresh runs report 1 versus 34/35 empty pose calls depending on the run. Earlier exact-image probes already
established empty results on these images. Neither the new low-kick rules nor the replay-clock correction
claims to repair that Vision behavior.

DECISION
Keep the two low-kick rules as isolated debug opt-ins. Use the precise replay mode for future paced
comparisons and retain source-admission audit logs. No production default, TestFlight or iPhone 11 change.
The next bounded app experiment should separate the regular detector clock from the old-frame retry
clock while preserving single-worker, age and backlog limits. Do not re-enable the broader source-grid
prototypes: earlier tests found legal-contact losses. Recheck the known low-kick and ground-negative
fixtures before any promotion. A better total by itself is insufficient.
