Conclusion and decision
The observed-descent measurement is a useful narrow recovery. Both low.mov candidate runs count 170
legal contacts, against 166 and 165 in the controls. Four specific legal contacts recur in both gains:
9.56, 18.13, 28.06, 57.44 seconds. Inputs within 200 ms of each contact are identical in each pair.
The controls reject amplitudeTooSmall/footConfirmed=false; the candidates meet the unchanged fall
threshold using actual measured descent and pass the subsequent contact checks. The fifth difference
in repeat 2 (54.08 s) is not an observed-fall proof and should not be credited to this mechanism.
No previously counted labelled low.mov contact is lost in either pair.

Same-input ablation removes the four proof contacts, but three disabled paths lack a recorded foot
callback. Keep that counterfactual limitation explicit. The fresh paired controls provide independent
rejection evidence for those same timestamps; do not silently fabricate missing callbacks.

IMG8874 remains 24 -> 24, holding and dribbling remain 0 -> 0. IMG8606 is 33 -> 35 legal contacts and retains
its existing false count at 20.176 s; no observed-fall proof qualifies there, so its two gains are not
credited to the new measurement.

The three-minute total 158 -> 157 hides three gained legal contacts (35.413, 54.280, 107.910 s) and four
lost legal contacts (28.365, 51.213, 53.046, 141.325 s). The six foot contacts were visually reviewed;
the knee contact at 107.910 was already reviewed. The new proof never qualifies in this clip.
Disabling it on the same captured inputs preserves all 157 counts with complete callbacks.
The changed events reflect different tracking/candidate/contact evidence between fresh runs:
missing candidate at 28.365/51.213; missingBall body rejections at 53.080/141.358; control ground or
unresolved-low rejection at 35.446/54.280; control track break at 107.943. This establishes no direct
numeric rejection by the new proof; it does not establish full live-pipeline stability.
Each three-minute run skipped one source frame. These are not zero-drop performance results.

Both arms still count the known ground bounce 154.206 s and the suspected false event 145.458 s.
Both reject 153.773 and157.807 s. The suspected event also appears in the old-clock control, so it
cannot be attributed uniquely to the previously tested clock experiment. None of the visually
reviewed newly added counts in this isolated pair is a ground bounce. This is partial review,
not a full-clip ground-truth count or a claim that ground false positives are solved.

Keep this measurement as a separate opt-in change; leave the clock experiment off. No defaults or
release settings are promoted. The next counting priority remains short legal kicks that never
produce a candidate (for example 4.67 s in low.mov), with tracking/impact evidence stability tracked
separately. Field build 2913 has the older startup gate; this test held experimental startup V3
constant in both arms. Test the ordinary startup configuration before any default promotion.
